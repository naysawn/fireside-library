# Core Messaging

## The three-part argument

1. It is hard to sustain deep conversations for hours. Most discussions lose focus, drift to the surface, or run out of steam. Even when the desire is there, keeping a group engaged on subjects of real depth is genuinely difficult.

2. Written materials make that easier. Having something to read together gives the conversation an anchor. It keeps the group focused, provides a shared reference point, and ensures the discussion stays on subjects of substance rather than wandering.

3. We do not learn by reading alone. The Faith is vast and beautiful, but we do not process it just by reading a text. We need to explore it together, to ask questions, to hear how others understand it, to sit with ideas that challenge us. That is what these modules are: materials designed for people to explore the Baha'i Faith together.

## How this translates to the site

The homepage should communicate this arc quickly and clearly:

- The problem: deep conversations are hard to sustain
- The insight: written material gives conversations structure and depth
- The product: modules designed for group exploration of the Baha'i Faith

The tone should not be salesy or promotional. It should feel like someone explaining why they made something, because it solved a real problem they kept running into.

## Character of the project

- This is open source. The modules, the process, the tools are all available for anyone to use, adapt, and improve.
- This started as an experiment in Boston. It is still an experiment. We need other communities to try these, give feedback, and help us learn what works.
- We are doing our best. This is not polished institutional output from a committee. It is a group of people who care deeply about teaching the Faith, trying something, and sharing what they have learned so far.
- The site should reflect this honesty. Not "we have all the answers" but "here is what we have built so far, and we would love your help making it better."
