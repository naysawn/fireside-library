# Core Messaging

## The three-part argument

1. It is hard to sustain deep conversations about subjects that matter. Most discussions lose focus, drift to the surface, or run out of steam. Even when the desire is there. Written materials make that easier. Having something to read together gives the conversation an anchor.

2. Questions cause people to think. Hearing from others opens up new ways of understanding.

3. We do not learn by reading alone. The Faith is vast and beautiful, but we do not process it just by reading a text. We need to explore it together, to ask questions, to hear how others understand it, to sit with ideas that challenge us. That is what these modules are: materials designed for people to explore the Baha'i Faith together.

## How this translates to the site

The homepage should communicate this arc quickly and clearly:

- The problem: deep conversations are hard to sustain
- The insight: written material gives conversations structure and depth
- The product: modules designed for group exploration of the Baha'i Faith

## Character of the project

- This is open source. The modules, the process, the tools are all available for anyone to use, adapt, and improve.
- This started as an experiment in Boston. It is still an experiment. We need other communities to try these, give feedback, and help us learn what works.
- We are doing our best. This is not polished institutional output from a committee. It is a group of people who care deeply about teaching the Faith, trying something, and sharing what they have learned so far.
- The site should reflect this honesty. Not "we have all the answers" but "here is what we have built so far, and we would love your help making it better."

## Voice

### The core principle

This site speaks the way a thoughtful, warm Baha'i would speak to a fellow Baha'i who is looking for better tools to share the Faith with seekers.

Not a pamphlet. Not a sales page. Not institutional communications. A genuine conversation with someone who built something useful and wants to share it.

### Who is speaking

The voice is the project itself, a small group of people who care about teaching the Faith. Not an institution, not a marketing department.

It speaks from a place of quiet confidence. These modules have been tested. They have sparked real conversations. But the project is not trying to prove itself or position against other approaches. It is simply describing something that has worked and inviting others to try it.

### Who is being spoken to

Baha'is who want to share the Faith with seekers and are looking for materials that actually work in practice. People who have sat in a living room with a curious friend and wished they had something to read together. People who have tried other approaches and are open to trying this one.

### Voice qualities

**Warm but not effusive.** Say what you mean directly. Do not oversell. The modules' value comes through in what they are and how they are described, not in exclamation marks or hype.

**Confident but not evangelical.** The project does not need to convince anyone. It describes what it offers, invites people to try it, and trusts them to decide. It never implies other teaching approaches are wrong or insufficient.

**Honest about what things are.** These are experimental. They started in Boston. They are open source. They are not perfect. Say all of this plainly.

**Specific over generic.** Real module titles, real session lengths, real outcomes ("three-hour discussions on deep questions"). Generalities feel like every other religious education website. Specifics feel real.

**Direct without being curt.** Short sentences are good. But warmth requires some breath. Find the balance: a knowledgeable friend describing something they genuinely love to someone they genuinely care about.

---

## What this site is not trying to do

**Not trying to sell anything.** The modules are free and open source. The site introduces them and makes them available. There is no conversion funnel.

**Not trying to compete.** These modules are not positioned against Anna's Presentation, Ruhi materials, firesides, or any other teaching approach. They are one more tool. If someone prefers other materials, that is fine.

**Not trying to explain everything.** The site gives visitors enough to understand what the modules are and decide if they want to try them. The urge to explain the entire philosophy behind them must be resisted.

**Not trying to be official.** This is not an institutional Baha'i website. It is an open source project from a group of Baha'is in Boston. The tone should never suggest official endorsement or institutional authority.

## Writing rules

### Patterns to avoid

**Em dashes.** Never use them. They are the single most reliable signal of AI-generated writing.

**The setup-payoff opener.** "When it comes to teaching the Faith, what matters most is..." Just say the thing.

**Throat-clearing transitions.** "It's worth noting that...", "It's important to remember that..." Cut them every time.

**Symmetrical three-part lists.** "Read, discuss, and grow." Real writing does not always come in threes. Vary the structure.

**The negation-definition pattern.** "Not a lecture. Not a class. A real discussion." Say what the thing is. A single comparison works. Three negations in a row never does.

**Adjective stacking.** "A rich, diverse, meaningful collection of deeply spiritual materials." Choose one good adjective or none.

**Words that mean nothing.** "Meaningful." "Impactful." "Intentional." "Holistic." "Authentic." Replace with a concrete description.

**The closing summary sentence.** If the paragraph did its job, the summary is redundant. Cut it.

**Passive voice.** "Modules are designed for group study." Who designed them? Use active voice.

### Words to avoid

- "Journey." Overused in spiritual contexts.
- "Transform" / "transformative." Evangelical connotation.
- "Vibrant." Means nothing.
- "Welcoming" as a standalone claim. Show it, do not claim it.
- "Explore your spirituality." Generic wellness language.
- "Delve." AI signature word.
- "Tapestry." AI signature word.
- "Multifaceted." AI signature word.

### The test

Read the sentence out loud. Would a real person say this to a friend? If it sounds like something from a nonprofit brochure, a corporate mission statement, or a wellness app onboarding screen, rewrite it.

## Formatting conventions

- No em dashes anywhere
- No diacritical marks on Baha'i names (Baha'u'llah not Bahá'u'lláh)
- Sans-serif typography throughout the site
- Vary sentence length. Long sentences establish context. Short ones land points.
