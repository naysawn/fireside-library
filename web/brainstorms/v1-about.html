About This Project

Teaching matters more than almost anything else

Baha'u'llah told His followers plainly: "God hath prescribed unto every one the duty of proclaiming His Message, and regardeth it as the most meritorious of all deeds." He wrote that entire Tablets were laden with this theme. "To assist Me is to teach My Cause."

Shoghi Effendi picked up that urgency and pressed it further. "Let us arise to teach His Cause with righteousness, conviction, understanding and vigor," he wrote. "Let us make it the dominating passion of our life." He called teaching "the most essential, the most urgent of all our obligations."

The Universal House of Justice calls teaching "the sacred act of assisting a soul to recognize and obey his Lord. It is a spiritual process, involving spiritual forces, spiritual preparation, and spiritual action."

That is the context for everything you see on this site. We built these modules because teaching is what we are supposed to be doing, and we wanted better tools to do it with.

"...this Body of humanity's material civilization calls aloud, yearns more desperately with each passing day, for its Soul. As with every great civilization in history, until it is so animated, and its spiritual faculties awakened, it will find neither peace, nor justice, nor a unity that rises above the level of negotiation and compromise."

Universal House of Justice

The House of Justice makes the stakes clear. The Baha'i community will keep contributing to efforts toward global betterment. But those contributions are secondary to its purpose. "Its purpose is to assist the people of the world to open their minds and hearts to the one Power that can fulfill their ultimate longing." Only those who have awakened to the Revelation of God can bring that help.

We take that seriously. These modules are our small attempt to do something about it.

Written materials work. We have seen it before.

This is not a new idea. Having written material to read together helps sustain conversation and keeps the focus on subjects worth discussing. People who study the history of Baha'i teaching in the Americas have seen this pattern more than once.

In Colombia, during the growth that eventually led to the creation of the Ruhi materials, seekers were taught using a booklet called Conozcamos La Fe Baha'i. It walked through the basics of the Faith on a one-on-one basis. It gave the conversation structure.

In the United States, the most rapid rise of enrollments came when communities used what Baha'is called the "Red Teaching Book." Travel teachers would sit down with people, open the book, and answer questions as they went through it together. The book kept the conversation focused, concrete, and personal.

We are trying to do something similar with these modules. Give a small group something to read together. Ask questions that cause people to think. Let the conversation go where it goes. In our experience, that combination has sparked three-hour discussions about suffering, the existence of God, what happens after death, and what it means to live a spiritual life in this century.

How a module works

Each module covers a single subject and fills a single session of two to three hours. A facilitator reads through the text with a small group, stopping at the questions along the way.

Two sections

Every module has two sections, usually with a break between them. The first section introduces the subject and establishes the core ideas. The second goes deeper, applies them, or shifts perspective. You can stop after the first section if the group runs long. Both sections stand on their own.

Reflection questions

These are woven into the readings. They ask people to pause and consider what they think. "What does it mean to you that human beings were created noble?" or "Have you ever felt like your prayers were heard?" They are not quiz questions. There are no wrong answers. They are invitations to think out loud.

Readings

Each module draws on scripture, writings from Baha'u'llah, Abdu'l-Baha, and Shoghi Effendi, and guidance from the Universal House of Justice. The text provides context and explanation. It is not a lecture. It is closer to what a knowledgeable friend might say if you asked them a big question over dinner.

Discussion questions

At the end of each section, broader questions open up the conversation. These are less about the text and more about what the group thinks. They are the part where people tend to forget what time it is.

How we write them

Each module goes through a process. It takes a while. We are trying to produce something worth reading, which means we do not rush it.

Planning We identify the subject, gather source materials, and outline the two sections. We figure out what questions the module should help someone think through.
Drafting A writer produces a first draft. We use AI to help generate an initial version, then a human writer reworks it into something that sounds like a person wrote it. The AI gives us speed. The human gives us voice.
Editorial An editor reviews the draft for accuracy, tone, flow, and faithfulness to the source material. This is where we catch things that sound generic or institutional.
Rewrite The writer revises based on editorial feedback. Sometimes this is light. Sometimes we start a section over.
Beta We release the module for use in real sessions. Facilitators try it with groups and tell us what worked and what fell flat.
Field testing We collect feedback from multiple sessions, look for patterns, and identify where the module needs improvement.
V1 After revisions based on field testing, the module reaches its first stable version. It still gets updated. But we feel good enough about it to recommend it.

Open source, open to help

Everything about this project is open. The modules themselves, the source materials, the writing process, and the code behind this website are all available on GitHub.

We did this because we want other communities to use these materials, adapt them, and tell us what they learned. If a module does not work well for your group, we want to know why. If you have a subject you think we should cover, tell us. If you are a writer or editor and want to help produce new modules, we would welcome that.

This project started with a handful of Baha'is in Boston. We hope it does not stay that way. The more communities that try these, the better they will get.

If you want to get involved, the best place to start is the GitHub repository. You can also reach us through the contact information there.
