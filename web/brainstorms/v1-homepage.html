Written materials for sharing the Baha'i Faith

Twelve modules on subjects that matter to seekers. Each one gives a small group something to read together, questions to sit with, and a reason to keep talking for three hours.

Why we built these

We kept running into the same problem. A friend asks about the Faith, and the conversation starts strong. But then it drifts. People get polite, or abstract, or the energy just runs out. The desire is there, but desire alone does not sustain a three-hour conversation about the nature of God.

Written materials change the dynamic. When you have something to read together, the conversation has an anchor. Questions give people a reason to think out loud. Hearing how someone else understands a passage opens up perspectives you would never get to on your own.

Conversations need structure

Even when the desire is there, deep conversations about subjects that matter tend to lose focus or drift to the surface. Having something to read together keeps the discussion grounded in ideas worth discussing.

Questions cause people to think

Each module weaves reflection questions into the readings. These are not discussion starters for the sake of participation. They ask people to consider what they actually believe about things like suffering, prayer, and the purpose of human life.

We learn by exploring together

The Faith is vast. We do not process it just by reading a text on our own. We need to hear how others understand it, to sit with ideas that challenge us, to say things out loud we have only half-formed in our heads. That is what these modules are for.

An experiment, not a finished product

This project started in Boston. A few of us wanted better materials for sharing the Faith with seekers, so we started writing them. We tested them in living rooms and over Zoom. Some worked well. Some we rewrote entirely. We are still learning what works.

Everything here is open source. The modules, the writing process, and the tools we use to produce them are all available on GitHub. We would love for other communities to try these, give us honest feedback, and help us figure out what we have not thought of yet.

We are not trying to replace any existing approach to teaching. Firesides, study circles, Anna's Presentation, one-on-one conversations: all of those work. These modules are one more tool. If they help, use them. If something else works better for your community, do that.
