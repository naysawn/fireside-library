About This Project

A small group of Baha'is in Boston built these modules because we wanted better tools for sharing the Faith. Here is why, and how.

Why teaching matters

At all times in our history, the Central Figures and Institutions of the Faith have described teaching as of paramount importance. It is the duty Baha'u'llah placed above all others.

"Teach ye the Cause of God, O people of Baha, for God hath prescribed unto every one the duty of proclaiming His Message, and regardeth it as the most meritorious of all deeds."

Baha'u'llah

Shoghi Effendi called on the American believers to make this the center of their lives.

"Let us arise to teach His Cause with righteousness, conviction, understanding and vigor. Let us make it the dominating passion of our life."

Shoghi Effendi

The Universal House of Justice describes teaching as "the sacred act of assisting a soul to recognize and obey his Lord. It is a spiritual process, involving spiritual forces, spiritual preparation, and spiritual action."

The world needs the Baha'i teachings. The Universal House of Justice puts it plainly:

"There are none, except those who have themselves awakened to the Revelation of God, who can bring this help."

Universal House of Justice

We built these modules because we take that responsibility seriously. We wanted materials that work in practice, that help real conversations happen between real people.

Why written materials

Experience has shown that having written material to read together helps sustain conversation and keeps the focus on subjects of real depth.

This is not a new insight. In Colombia, during the growth that eventually led to the creation of the Ruhi materials, seekers were taught using a booklet called Conozcamos La Fe Baha'i, which walks through the basics of the Faith one-on-one. The most rapid rise of enrollments in the United States occurred when communities leveraged the "Red Teaching Book" as material for people to look at while travel teachers answered questions.

Written text gives a conversation structure. Questions cause people to think. Hearing from others opens up ways of understanding you would never reach alone. We do not process the Faith just by reading. We need to explore it together, to ask questions, to sit with ideas that challenge us. That is what these modules create the space for.

How we make them

Every module goes through the same process. We take it seriously because the quality of the text directly affects the quality of the conversation it produces.

Planning

We identify a subject, research the Baha'i writings on it, and outline what the module needs to cover. We pull quotations and organize them into a structure that builds understanding section by section.

Drafting

A writer produces the first draft, weaving the quotations into readable passages and writing reflection and discussion questions. We use AI tools to help generate initial drafts, but a human writer revises every word.

Review

Other writers and editors read the draft and give feedback. We check the quotations against source texts. We cut anything that feels like filler.

Rewrite

The writer produces a new version based on feedback. Most modules go through two or three rounds of revision before testing.

Beta testing

We use the module in real sessions with real seekers. We watch where conversations catch fire and where they stall. We take notes on what works and what does not.

Version 1

After testing and final revisions, the module reaches v1. It is ready for other communities to use. But we keep learning from feedback and will continue to improve them.

Open source. Come help.

Everything about this project is open. The modules, the writing process, and the tools we use to produce them are all on GitHub. We are not a committee or an institution. We are a small group of people who care about teaching the Faith, trying something, and sharing what we have learned so far.

We need other communities to try these modules. We need writers, editors, and people willing to facilitate sessions and tell us what happened. If something here is useful to you, take it. If you want to help make it better, we would love to hear from you.
