Texts to maker it easier to teach

Having written material to read together helps sustain conversation and keeps the focus on subjects worth discussing. 

Without looking at something together, things drift, we talk about the weather, we talk about sports. Anything to keep from talking about subjects of real meaning! 

Give a small group something to read together. Ask questions that cause people to think. Let the conversation go where it goes. 

In our experience, that combination has sparked three-hour discussions about suffering, the existence of God, what happens after death, and what it means to live a spiritual life in this century.

# About Page 

Section 

Text to sustain deep discussions

Each module covers a single subject and fills a single session of two to three hours. Groups of 4-8 people work best. Large enough for conversation from multiple sources, small enough where everyone can speak.

Two sections

Every module has two or three sections. The first section introduces the subject and establishes the core ideas. The second goes deeper, applies them, or shifts perspective. The third sometimes exists if it just feel impossible to cover the topic in two only. You can stop after the first section if the group runs long. They each are meant to stand on their own. 

Opening questions

Each section asks people to open themselves up and share. "Have you ever felt like your prayers were heard?" "Have you ever felt the presence of the divine", etc. They are invitations to think out loud. We learn also hearing from everyone else. 

Readings

Each module draws on scripture, writings from Baha'u'llah, Abdu'l-Baha, and Shoghi Effendi, and guidance from the Universal House of Justice. The text provides context and explanation. The readings assume that participants don't subscribe to the teachings but have a good working knowledge of the Faith.

Discussion questions

At the end of each section, more questions are there to again consider the topic from other angles. Learning often requires contemplation, meditation and questions can open These are less about the text and more about what the group thinks. They are the part where people tend to forget what time it is.
