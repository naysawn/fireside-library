# Audience

## The Setting

Each module is designed for a small group study session lasting a couple of hours. The typical group is 2 Baha'is and 2 people exploring the Baha'i Faith (seekers). The module is a discussion tool, not standalone reading material. It provides the scaffold; the group provides the conversation.

## Seekers

- May have no prior knowledge of the Baha'i Faith
- Come from diverse religious backgrounds or none at all
- Are genuinely curious — they've chosen to be in the room
- May have specific questions from their own life experience that brought them to explore
- Should never feel talked down to, preached at, or expected to agree

## Baha'is

- Range from newly declared to lifelong Baha'is
- Are not teachers — they are co-learners in the discussion
- May not have deep knowledge of every topic covered
- Their role is to participate honestly, not to have all the answers

## Personas by Phase

Each module is tagged with a seeker phase. The phase determines who is likely in the room and what the module can assume.

### Discovery — "The Curious Visitor"

Someone who showed up because a friend invited them, or because they are going through something and are open to exploring. They may or may not believe in God. They have no Baha'i vocabulary. They are deciding whether this feels safe and genuine, or whether they are being recruited.

**What they are thinking:**
- "Is this going to be preachy?"
- "Do I have to believe something to be here?"
- "Will I be able to say what I actually think?"
- "Is this a group that respects my intelligence?"

**What the module must do:**
- Assume nothing: no God, no soul, no scripture as authority
- Open with universal human experience, not Baha'i concepts
- Frame everything as "one perspective worth considering"
- Leave every question genuinely open
- The reader should feel invited, never instructed

**Current modules:** 08 (Prayer)

### Investigation — "The Active Explorer"

Someone who has attended a few gatherings and is genuinely interested. They know the basics: who Baha'u'llah is, roughly what Baha'is believe. They are deciding whether this Faith holds up under scrutiny. They are comparing it to what they already believe. They are reading on their own between sessions.

**What they are thinking:**
- "This is interesting, but does it really work?"
- "What are the hard parts? What is not being said?"
- "How is this different from what I already believe?"
- "Can I trust this community with my real questions?"

**What the module must do:**
- Can assume basic familiarity (who Baha'u'llah is, progressive revelation)
- Must still frame claims as the Baha'i understanding, not self-evident truth
- Should actively surface the hard questions a skeptic would ask
- Can go deeper into scripture but must explain why it matters
- The reader should feel respected as an independent thinker

**Current modules:** 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 09, 11, 12

### Consolidation — "The New Baha'i"

Someone who has recognized Baha'u'llah and is beginning to identify as a Baha'i. They want to understand how to participate in community life. They are still forming their understanding of many teachings, but they have made a commitment. They may feel overwhelmed by the scope of what they have joined.

**What they are thinking:**
- "OK, I believe this. Now how does this actually work?"
- "What is expected of me as part of this community?"
- "How do I explain this to my family and friends?"
- "Some of this is still hard. Is it OK to say that?"

**What the module must do:**
- Can assume the reader accepts Baha'u'llah's station
- Can use Baha'i vocabulary without glossing every term
- Should still be warm and conversational, not institutional
- Can address "how to" questions and community practice
- The reader should feel welcomed into something they are part of

**Current modules:** 10 (Administrative Order)

## What This Means for the Writing

- Assume nothing about what the reader knows (discovery), or assume basic familiarity (investigation/consolidation)
- Briefly gloss Baha'i terms and concepts when they first appear
- Ground spiritual ideas in universal human experience
- The tone is warm and conversational — like a thoughtful friend explaining something, not a lecture
- Quotations from the Writings should feel like a natural part of the conversation, not proof texts
- Questions should open genuine exploration, not lead to predetermined answers
- The module is a scaffold for discussion, not a standalone argument. The Baha'is in the room provide context, warmth, and honest engagement that the text alone cannot
