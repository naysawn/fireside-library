Getting Human Writing from Claude

Research-backed best practices for the Baha'i module project. How to brief Claude, set examples, and grade output.

1. Why AI Writing Sounds Like AI

Before fixing the problem, understand the root cause. Claude defaults to "corporate helpful": a blend of Wikipedia neutrality and LinkedIn consulting speak. This happens because safety training rewards blandness, and the internet's professional writing is already templated. The result is text that is fluent, correct, and completely devoid of personality.

The AI Voice Fingerprint

Vocabulary tells

delvetapestrymultifacetedlandscape journeybeaconcornerstoneinterplay leverageelevatefosterharness underscoreshowcasebolstergarner moreoverfurthermoreindeednotably subsequentlyaccordinglygroundbreaking seamlessrobustgame changer

Structural tells

  • Uniform sentence length (~27 words, no variation)
  • Compulsive rule-of-three (three adjectives, three examples, three bullets)
  • Excessive em dashes (already in your style guide)
  • Perfect grammar: no fragments, no sentences starting with "And" or "But"
  • Copula avoidance: "serves as," "stands as," "represents" instead of "is"
  • Formulaic conclusions that restate what was already said
  • Every paragraph the same length

What Human Writers Do Differently

The things AI cannot fake

  • Earned specificity. A human writes "12:34 p.m." where AI writes "around noon." Details that could only come from lived experience.
  • Controlled imperfection. Sentence fragments. Sentences starting with "And." Stylistic quirks. The roughness readers enjoy.
  • Genuine surprise. Bold opinions, creative risks, an analogy that comes from left field. AI plays it safe.
  • Asymmetric rhythm. A three-word sentence after a fifty-word one. Paragraph breaks that serve emotion, not just organization.
  • Tolerated tangents. A sentence that eases into a side thought, then returns with a payoff. AI smooths these away.
  • Emotional honesty without performance. Vulnerability that feels real, not "performatively literary."
Key finding: A study of 556 participants found that identical literary passages received higher ratings when labeled "human-written" vs. "AI-generated." Features praised as "imaginative" under human labels were dismissed as "formulaic" under AI labels. This means AI writing must clear a higher bar to be perceived as good. You cannot afford any AI tells.

2. How to Brief Claude for the Modules

The research points to a three-part system: Voice DNA (how the writing should sound), Audience Profile (who is reading), and Anti-Patterns (what to avoid). You already have the audience profile in audience.md and anti-patterns in style.md. What is missing is the Voice DNA.

The Voice DNA Document

Build this from 3-5 of your best paragraphs (Module 08 v2 is a start). It should capture:

Element What to Document Example from Module 08 v2
Sentence rhythm How do you mix short and long sentences? What is the typical range? "Prayer, in the Baha'i understanding, is not a means of informing God of anything." (15 words) followed by a longer explanatory sentence. Short statement, then expansion.
How you introduce quotes Do you set up quotes with context? Drop them in? Use them as section anchors? Quotes are woven into the flow: context sentence, then quote, then what it means for the reader. Never just "As Baha'u'llah says:"
Analogy style Where do your analogies come from? How extended are they? Nature: dew on ground, riverbed shaped by water, hollow reed. Brief (1-2 sentences), concrete, no over-explaining.
Paragraph openings How do you start paragraphs? With a claim? A question? A continuation? Often with a quiet observation: "There is something in most people that reaches beyond the material." Not with transitions like "Furthermore" or "Additionally."
How you handle difficulty When the topic is hard or the Baha'i position is counter-cultural, what do you do? Name the tension directly: "Why pray if God already knows what is in our hearts?" Then sit with it before offering the Baha'i perspective.
Words you use / avoid Beyond the banned list, what is your natural vocabulary? Uses: "something," "sense of," "impulse," "draw closer," "in this understanding." Avoids: technical theological terms, academic register.
The single most effective technique: Record yourself talking about the module topic for 5 minutes. Transcribe it. Feed the transcription to Claude as voice reference. Your speaking voice is closer to your real writing voice than your "trying to write" voice. The transcription captures your natural rhythm, your real vocabulary, and the way you actually explain things.

3. Prompting Techniques That Work

What the Research Says

  • Match prompt style to output style. If you want flowing prose, write your prompt in flowing prose. Removing markdown from your prompt reduces markdown in the output.
  • Use 3-5 examples. Few-shot prompting dramatically improves voice consistency. Show Claude 3 paragraphs that sound right before asking it to write.
  • Say why, not just what. "Never use em dashes" is less effective than "Em dashes are a signature AI tell and readers will immediately distrust the text."
  • Force a stance. Instead of "write about prayer," say "make the case that prayer is not about informing God but about transforming the one who prays."
  • Enforce specificity. "Every claim must include an analogy, a quote, or a concrete example. No vague benefits."
  • Separate tasks. Use different passes for research synthesis, drafting, and copyediting. One prompt doing everything produces mush.

What Your Style Guide Already Does Well

  • Banned word list (can be expanded)
  • Em dash prohibition
  • "Not X but Y" prohibition
  • Block quote requirements (structural constraint that forces integration)
  • Acceptance criteria checklist

What to Add

  • The Voice DNA document (see above)
  • Expanded banned patterns: copula avoidance, rule-of-three, formulaic transitions, summary conclusions
  • A "read aloud" test with specific criteria (see rubric below)
  • Sentence length variation requirement: "No three consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other in length"
  • Permission for imperfection: "Sentence fragments are OK for emphasis. Start sentences with 'And' or 'But' when it serves rhythm."

Example: How to Prompt a Module Draft

# System prompt (in SKILL.md or CLAUDE.md) You are writing a reading passage for a Baha'i study module. The audience is the Active Explorer: someone who has been to a few gatherings, knows who Baha'u'llah is, and is deciding whether this Faith holds up under scrutiny. They should feel respected as an independent thinker. ## Voice Write like a thoughtful friend explaining something over tea. Not a lecture. Not a sales pitch. Not a Wikipedia article. Read these three paragraphs. Match this rhythm, this level of directness, this way of introducing quotes: [paste 3 paragraphs from Module 08 v2 or your best writing] ## What to do - Open with a genuine question or honest observation, not a topic sentence - Use short analogies from nature (1-2 sentences, concrete, not extended) - Weave quotes into the flow: context, then quote, then what it means for the reader. Never drop a quote and move on. - Name the hard questions before offering the Baha'i perspective - Vary sentence length. Mix 8-word sentences with 35-word ones. No three consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other. ## What to avoid - Em dashes. Use commas, periods, or semicolons. - Any word from this list: delve, tapestry, multifaceted, landscape, journey, beacon, cornerstone, moreover, furthermore, indeed, notably, it is worth noting, let us unpack, in essence, at its core - "Not X, not Y, but Z" constructions - Copula avoidance: do not write "serves as," "stands as," "represents" when you mean "is" - Rule of three: do not list three adjectives, three examples, or three parallel clauses unless the content genuinely demands it - Summary conclusions that restate what you already said - Contractions (write "do not" not "don't") ## Format - 500-750 words - At least 3 block quotes with [n] citation numbers - Inline quotes italicized - Citations section at the end

4. Before and After: What Changes

AI Default

Prayer is a multifaceted spiritual practice that serves as a cornerstone of the Baha'i Faith. It is worth noting that Baha'u'llah emphasized the transformative power of prayer, which fosters a deeper connection with the Divine. Moreover, prayer serves as a means of spiritual development, enabling individuals to cultivate qualities such as patience, gratitude, and compassion.

After Voice DNA + Anti-Patterns

There is something in most people that reaches beyond the material. A pull toward gratitude, or a weight we want to lay down, or a sense that something larger is listening. Baha'u'llah describes this impulse simply: prayer is food for the soul. Not information for God. Not a transaction. Food. Something the soul needs the way a body needs water.

AI Default

The Baha'i Administrative Order represents a groundbreaking system of governance that stands as a unique contribution to human civilization. It is characterized by three levels of elected councils — Local Spiritual Assemblies, National Spiritual Assemblies, and the Universal House of Justice — each serving as pillars of a divinely ordained framework.

After Voice DNA + Anti-Patterns

Something is breaking down in the institutions people once trusted. Religious institutions, governments, international bodies. The gap between what they promise and what they deliver grows wider each year. Baha'is believe this is not an accident but a symptom: humanity has outgrown the systems built for an earlier stage of its collective life. So what replaces them?

AI Default

Baha'u'llah, the Founder of the Baha'i Faith, endured decades of suffering and exile, which underscores the depth of His commitment to His divine mission. His journey from Tehran to Akka — spanning over 2,500 miles — serves as a testament to His unwavering dedication.

After Voice DNA + Anti-Patterns

Baha'u'llah spent forty years as a prisoner. He was poisoned, chained in an underground dungeon, exiled four times across 2,500 miles, and stripped of everything a person could lose. And during those years He wrote. Constantly. Over 20,000 works on subjects ranging from the nature of God to the governance of nations. Whatever you make of His claim, the fact of His life is extraordinary.

5. The Grading Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate drafts. It is designed to be used by Claude as an objective judge, with specific counter-measures for the biases LLMs have when evaluating their own writing.

Critical anti-bias measure: Never have the same conversation generate AND evaluate. Start a fresh context for judging. Include examples of polished-but-empty AI text that should score LOW. This forces the judge to see past surface fluency.
Dimension Weight 1 (Weak) 3 (Adequate) 5 (Strong)
1. Specificity
Concrete detail vs. vague claims		 High Abstract language throughout. "Prayer is important." No examples, no mechanisms, no sensory detail. Some concrete details. 1-2 analogies. Most claims have at least one supporting example. Rich in earned detail. Analogies from nature, specific quotes woven in, mechanisms explained. Every paragraph has something concrete.
2. Voice
Distinctive human sound		 High Could be anyone. Generic helpful tone. Uses AI vocabulary. Sounds like a Wikipedia article crossed with a brochure. Has a recognizable warmth. Mostly avoids AI tells. Reads as "a person wrote this" but without a strong individual voice. Sounds like a specific person. Has mannerisms, a rhythm, an attitude. You would recognize this writer in a blind test.
3. Rhythm Variation
Sentence length diversity		 Medium Most sentences 20-30 words. No variation. Monotone hum. Reading aloud feels like a metronome. Some variation. A few short sentences. But overall pattern is predictable. Deliberate mix: 5-word punches and 40-word flowing sentences. Paragraph lengths vary. Reading aloud has natural cadence.
4. Structural Courage
Claims without hedging		 Medium Every claim qualified: "may," "typically," "it could be argued," "some would say." No commitments. Makes clear statements most of the time. Occasional unnecessary hedging. Direct claims backed by evidence or framed honestly as perspective. Uses "Baha'is understand this as..." rather than "it may be the case that..."
5. AI Pattern Absence
The anti-bias dimension		 High 3+ AI tells present: banned vocabulary, rule-of-three, em dashes, copula avoidance, formulaic transitions, summary conclusion. 1-2 minor AI tells. No banned words, but some structural predictability. Zero AI tells. Text could not be identified as AI-generated by any pattern-matching. Reads as fully human.
6. Emotional Honesty
Real feeling vs. performed feeling		 Medium Stock phrases: "truly transformative," "deeply meaningful." Emotion is declared, not earned. Some genuine moments. Acknowledges complexity or difficulty at least once. Names hard questions honestly. Admits where the Baha'i position is demanding. Vulnerability that feels real, not decorative.
7. Discussion Readiness
Works as a group tool		 High Too long to read aloud. No natural pause points. Questions lead to predetermined answers. Module does all the talking. Reasonable length. Some good questions. Could work in a group with effort. Perfect length for reading aloud with time for conversation. Questions genuinely open. At least one moment where the group will want to stop and talk.

6. Applying This to Your Existing Style Guide

Your style.md is already good. Here is what to add based on this research:

  1. Expand the banned patterns list. Add: copula avoidance ("serves as," "stands as," "represents" when you mean "is"), rule-of-three compulsion, formulaic transitions ("Additionally," "Consequently," "It is important to note"), summary conclusions that restate content, and uniform paragraph length.
  2. Add a sentence rhythm requirement. "No three consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other in length. Mix short declaratives (under 10 words) with longer flowing sentences (25+ words). Read aloud: if it sounds like a metronome, rewrite."
  3. Add permission for imperfection. "Sentence fragments are acceptable for emphasis. Sentences may begin with 'And' or 'But' when it serves rhythm. Not every paragraph needs to be the same length."
  4. Create the Voice DNA document. Build it from Module 08 v2 and from recordings of yourself explaining module topics aloud. Put it in brief/voice.md. Include 3-5 exemplar paragraphs with annotations explaining what makes them work.
  5. Add the grading rubric. Put the 7-dimension rubric in brief/rubric.md with the calibration examples. Use it as a gate before any draft advances past the editorial review phase.
  6. Separate Claude's tasks. Do not ask one prompt to research, draft, and polish. Use the interview phase (already in your SKILL.md) for research and direction, then a dedicated drafting prompt with Voice DNA, then a separate editing/grading pass in a fresh context.
  7. Add a "source of difficulty" requirement. Every module must contain at least one moment where the text names a hard question, a tension, or a counter-argument. "If the module does not make the reader slightly uncomfortable at least once, it is not being honest enough."

7. Quick Reference: The Anti-AI Checklist

Before Submitting a Draft, Check:

  • Search for every word on the banned list. Zero tolerance.
  • Count consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other. Flag clusters.
  • Search for em dashes. Remove all.
  • Search for "serves as," "stands as," "represents." Replace with "is."
  • Count instances of rule-of-three. Keep only those that genuinely need three items.
  • Check the last paragraph. If it restates the opening, delete it.
  • Read the first sentence of every paragraph. If they all sound the same (topic sentence, topic sentence, topic sentence), rewrite half of them.
  • Read the whole thing aloud. Where do you stumble? Where does it bore you? Those are the problems.

The 5-Second Test

Show a paragraph to someone without context. Ask:

  • "Did a person or a computer write this?"
  • "Could you describe the writer's personality?"
  • "What is the single most specific thing in this paragraph?"

If they say "computer," cannot describe a personality, or cannot point to anything specific, the paragraph needs rewriting.

The Attribution Test

Research shows identical text is rated lower when people think AI wrote it. Your text must be good enough to survive being suspected. If a reader thinks "this might be AI" and keeps reading anyway because the content is too specific, too surprising, or too honest to dismiss, you have won.
