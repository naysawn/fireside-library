Critical Review: Module Gap Analysis

A skeptic's reading of all 12 modules, measured against the project brief. April 2, 2026.

Methodology: Each module was read twice: once as standalone text, and once as a discussion tool for a small group (2 Baha'is + 2 seekers, ~2 hours). Scoring is calibrated to each module's phase. Discovery modules (M08) are held to the highest bar: assume nothing, frame everything as invitation. Investigation modules (most of the collection) can assume basic familiarity but must still frame claims as Baha'i perspective, not self-evident truth. Consolidation modules (M10) can assume the reader accepts Baha'u'llah. Scores also account for the module's role as a discussion scaffold: the Baha'is in the room provide context the text does not need to carry alone. Scored on 8 dimensions (1 = major gap, 5 = strong alignment with brief).

5. The Skeptic's Top Concerns (Cross-Cutting)

These are patterns that recur across multiple modules. A thoughtful skeptic attending these study circles would notice all of them.

A. "The text assumes its own conclusion"

Modules frequently present Baha'i theological claims as self-evident truths rather than propositions worth examining. Examples:

  • Mod 02: Presents the soul, life after death, and spiritual growth practices as facts. A seeker who is unsure whether they believe in God is immediately behind.
  • Mod 05: The miraculous survival of the Bab during the first volley is narrated as established fact with no historiographic framing.
  • Mod 07: Claims all modern progress is a "consequence of Baha'u'llah's revelation." This is an extraordinary claim that needs extraordinary scaffolding.
  • Mod 06: "Innate knowledge" of Abdu'l-Baha is stated plainly. A skeptic will ask: how do you know that?

Brief says: "Seekers should never feel preached at." Stating faith claims as facts is preaching, even in a warm tone.

B. "Quantity is presented as proof"

Multiple modules cite the volume of Baha'u'llah's writings (20,000 works, 7 million words) as implicit evidence of divine station. A skeptic notes:

  • Volume does not equal truth. L. Ron Hubbard was also prolific.
  • The comparison works as a point of interest but not as an argument.
  • Appears in: Modules 01, 05, and referenced indirectly in 07.

Fix: Present volume as remarkable and worth exploring, not as evidence. "Baha'u'llah produced an extraordinary body of work" vs. "This proves..."

C. "The hard questions are absent"

The modules avoid or gloss over the exact questions a skeptic would ask first:

  • Mod 06: UHJ is composed only of men. This is not addressed or contextualized at all. A skeptic sees evasion.
  • Mod 10: "No nominations, no campaigning" — but what about accountability? Dissent? How does this actually work in practice?
  • Mod 07: Blames "atheism" and "materialism" as dark forces. Many seekers are atheists or materialists. This is alienating.
  • Mod 12: Political non-involvement in the face of injustice — the tension is real but unexamined.
  • Mod 09: Symbolic interpretation of prophecy. A skeptic notes you can "fulfill" anything symbolically.

Brief says: "Baha'is are co-learners, not teachers." Co-learners engage with hard questions. Teachers avoid them.

D. "The tone is warm but the framing is closed"

This is the subtlest issue. The modules sound inviting but are structurally closed — they present one framework and one conclusion. A genuinely open text would:

  • Name the counter-position honestly before presenting the Baha'i view
  • Use language like "Baha'is understand this as..." rather than "The truth is..."
  • Include discussion questions that genuinely permit disagreement
  • Acknowledge where the Baha'i position is demanding or counter-cultural

Module 08 (v2) does this best — "Why pray if God already knows what we need?" is an honest opening. More modules need this energy.

E. "Suffering is romanticized"

Several modules frame historical suffering as evidence of legitimacy:

  • Mod 05: Story of Badi (17-year-old messenger tortured to death) — presented with reverence but no acknowledgment that glorifying a teenager's martyrdom can read as disturbing.
  • Mod 05: Baha'u'llah's suffering framed as proof of sincerity. Skeptic notes: many people have suffered for false causes.
  • Mod 07: Current world suffering described as "travail of childbirth" — this metaphor minimizes real suffering to people experiencing it.

F. "Two modules are not modules"

Modules 11 and 12 are complete letters from Baha'i institutions pasted with light framing:

  • They do not follow the module format at all (no proper opening questions, reading passages, closing questions)
  • The language is institutional, not conversational
  • Reading level is well above 10th grade
  • Module 12 has comprehension questions — this reads like a catechism, not a study circle
  • A skeptic sees these as "here, read the official position" rather than "let us explore this together"

Fix: These topics deserve real modules. Use the letters as source material and write proper modules around them, integrating quotes within a conversational reading passage.

7. Per-Module Skeptic's Notes

Module 01 — Overview of the Baha'i Faith Tier 3

  • Strongest aspect: Covers a lot of ground accessibly. The story of Baha'u'llah is told with warmth.
  • Skeptic's issue: "Three onenesses" framing is catchy but reductive. Progressive revelation is asserted, not explored. A skeptic asks: why should I accept that all religions come from the same source?
  • Fix: Frame progressive revelation as the Baha'i understanding, invite the reader to sit with it. Add a question like: "What would it mean if this were true? What difficulties does this idea raise?"

Module 02 — Spiritual Principles Tier 2

  • Skeptic's issue: This is the most assumption-heavy module. It presents the soul, the afterlife, and spiritual practices as established facts. A seeker who is agnostic or materialist has no on-ramp here.
  • Skeptic's issue: The "growing spiritually" section is a checklist of Baha'i practices (prayer, fasting, self-accounting). For a discovery-phase module, this reads like onboarding materials.
  • Fix: Open with the universal human experience of feeling there is "more" to life. Present the soul concept as one framework for understanding that feeling. Separate the practices section — a seeker needs to care why before hearing how.

Module 03 — Social Teachings Tier 3

  • Strongest aspect: The most accessible and least "religious" module. Social teachings have natural appeal.
  • Skeptic's issue: Tries to cover 10+ principles in one module. Each gets surface treatment. A skeptic says: these are nice ideas, but anyone could say this — what is the Baha'i mechanism for achieving them?
  • Skeptic's issue: Way too long. Exceeds word count targets significantly.
  • Fix: Split into two modules or choose 4-5 principles to explore deeply. Add substance: how do Baha'is actually implement these? What has worked? What is still a struggle?

Module 04 — Developing a Relationship with God Tier 3

  • Strongest aspect: Opening questions are the most universal in the collection. The kingdoms analogy is effective.
  • Skeptic's issue: Assumes the reader believes in God. The "unknowable essence" paired with "signs of God everywhere" is a tension that is not addressed — a skeptic sees contradiction.
  • Fix: Acknowledge that "developing a relationship with God" presupposes something the reader may still be exploring. The kingdoms analogy can work even for someone unsure — lean into it as a thought experiment.

Module 05 — The Bab and Baha'u'llah Tier 2

  • Strongest aspect: Compelling storytelling. The Bab's declaration scene and Baha'u'llah's exiles are vivid.
  • Skeptic's issue: The most hagiographic module. The firing squad miracle is presented as fact. Volume of writings used as evidence. Story of Badi romanticizes a teenager's death. Throughout, suffering = legitimacy, an argument form that proves too much.
  • Fix: Tell the stories but flag what is historical consensus vs. Baha'i tradition. Let the reader draw their own conclusions about what the stories mean. Replace "this proves" energy with "what do you make of this?" energy.

Module 06 — Centers of the Covenant Tier 2

  • Skeptic's issue: Reads like institutional PR. "Innate knowledge" is a major claim offered without support. Succession disputes (Covenant-breaking) are absent — a skeptic researching independently will find this and wonder why it was not mentioned.
  • Skeptic's issue: The UHJ section states it is composed of men without any discussion. This is the single biggest red flag for progressive-minded seekers. Silence reads as evasion.
  • Fix: Acknowledge that the male-only UHJ composition raises questions. You do not need to resolve the question — just signal that the Faith is aware the question exists. Similarly, briefly acknowledge succession challenges; the Covenant is stronger for being tested.

Module 07 — Making Sense of a Turbulent World Tier 1

  • Skeptic's issue: The most problematic module. Claims all modern progress stems from Baha'u'llah's revelation. Labels atheism and materialism as "dark forces." The May 23 1844 coincidence is presented as meaningful — a skeptic calls this cherry-picking.
  • Skeptic's issue: "Travail of childbirth" metaphor for world suffering. To someone who has actually suffered, this can feel dismissive.
  • Skeptic's issue: Way too long. Dense with claims. Reads as the most "insider" module — assumes the reader already finds the Baha'i worldview compelling.
  • Fix: This module needs fundamental rethinking. Start with the universal experience of living through turbulent times. Present the Baha'i framework as one lens. Do not blame skepticism itself as a dark force when your audience may include skeptics. Shorten significantly.

Module 08 — Prayer (v2) Tier 4 — Model

  • Strongest module overall. Opens with an honest question ("Why pray if God already knows?"). Uses nature analogies effectively. Follows the format. Warm without being preachy.
  • Remaining issue: Still assumes the reader prays or wants to. Could benefit from one more beat of acknowledgment: "If you do not pray, or are not sure what prayer means to you, that is a fine place to start."
  • Writer's flag: %%FLAG: I don't like the prayer is not information for God%% — this should be resolved.

Module 09 — The Baha'i Faith and Christianity Tier 3

  • Skeptic's issue: Uses biblical tests to validate Baha'u'llah — but this is circular for anyone who does not already accept the Bible as authoritative. Symbolic interpretation of prophecy is convenient: you can "fulfill" anything symbolically.
  • Skeptic's issue: Assumes the audience is Christian. Non-Christians in the study circle have no entry point.
  • Fix: Frame clearly as "for those coming from a Christian background." Acknowledge the symbolic interpretation concern directly: "You may notice that the Baha'i reading of these prophecies is symbolic rather than literal. This raises a fair question..."

Module 10 — Administrative Order Tier 3

  • Strongest aspect: The redefinition of power is genuinely interesting and counter-cultural.
  • Skeptic's issue: Presents a governance system without discussing accountability, dissent, or failure modes. "No nominations, no campaigning" sounds appealing but a skeptic asks: how do you prevent groupthink? What happens when a Local Assembly makes a bad decision?
  • Fix: Acknowledge that this system sounds unusual and invite the reader to sit with both its appeal and its tensions. Honest engagement with limitations builds more trust than a clean pitch.

Module 11 — Suffering and Justice Tier 1

  • Core problem: This is not a module. It is a letter from a Baha'i institution with framing and comprehension questions. The format, reading level, and voice do not match the brief at all.
  • Skeptic's issue: "Remote regions having their own unnamed Prophets" is a major theological claim dropped without exploration.
  • Fix: Rewrite as a proper module using the letter as source material. The topic — why does suffering exist if God is just? — is among the most important for seekers. It deserves the full treatment.

Module 12 — Non-Involvement in Partisan Politics Tier 1

  • Core problem: Same as Module 11 — a pasted letter, not a module. Comprehension questions read like a test.
  • Skeptic's issue: Political non-involvement in the face of injustice is a genuinely difficult position. The module does not wrestle with it — it states it. A skeptic asks: is this complicity? What about moral obligation?
  • Fix: Rewrite as a proper module. Open with the universal frustration of feeling the political system is broken. Present the Baha'i approach as a radical alternative, but honestly engage with the objection that stepping back from politics means stepping back from justice.

9. Recommended Next Steps

  1. Establish Module 08 (v2) as the benchmark. Put it in brief/samples/ (which is currently empty). Every future module should be measured against it for tone, structure, and skeptic-friendliness.
  2. Rewrite Modules 11 and 12 from scratch. These are letters, not modules. Use the letters as source material and write proper modules with the prescribed format. The topics are important — they deserve the same treatment as Prayer.
  3. Fundamentally rethink Module 07. This is the module most likely to alienate a skeptical seeker. The claims are too large, the framing is too closed, and labeling atheism as a "dark force" is counterproductive when your audience may include atheists. Consider splitting into two modules: one on living through turbulent times, one on the Baha'i vision of the future.
  4. Add "skeptic scaffolding" to Modules 02, 05, and 06. These are the next-most-assumption-heavy modules. For each, identify the 2-3 biggest claims and add framing language: "Baha'is understand this as..." or "This raises a natural question..." Acknowledge the hard questions (especially UHJ gender composition in Module 06).
  5. Create a "skeptic's checklist" for the editorial review phase. Add to the brief: before a module advances past editorial review, ask: (1) Does this module assume the reader already agrees? (2) Are the opening questions genuinely universal? (3) Are claims framed as Baha'i perspective or as self-evident truth? (4) Would a thoughtful atheist feel respected reading this?
  6. Run style guide cleanup across all V1 drafts. Remove em dashes, add citations sections, check word counts, verify block quote minimums. This is mechanical work that can be done in one pass.
  7. Tighten Module 03. It tries to cover too much. Either split it or choose 4-5 principles for depth over breadth. The social teachings are the easiest entry point for skeptics — do not waste this opportunity on a survey.
  8. Draft a "How to use these modules" guide for facilitators. Include guidance on: how to handle pushback and tough questions, how to create space for disagreement, how to avoid the "teacher-student" dynamic the brief warns against. The modules alone cannot solve the tone problem — the facilitation matters too.
