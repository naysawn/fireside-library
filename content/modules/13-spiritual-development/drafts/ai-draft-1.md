# Spiritual Development

## Section 1: A Dusty Mirror

### Questions for Reflection

*(to be drafted after both reading passages are approved)*

### Reading

The Baha'i teachings describe human beings as having a twofold nature. We have a higher, spiritual nature, capable of reflecting God's attributes such as kindness, generosity, justice, and love. And we have an egotistical nature, pulled toward self-interest and the satisfaction of immediate desires. Both are real. But our true self is the higher one. The lower nature is what we have to detach ourselves from; it is not who we most fundamentally are.

This lower nature goes by many names. Baha'i writings call it the insistent self. Other traditions call it the ego, or the whisperings of Satan. Shoghi Effendi tells us left unchecked, it can become *"a monster of selfishness, brutality, lust and so on."* Spiritual development is the ongoing work of subordinating the lower nature to the higher nature.

This struggle is hard at the level of an individual. It is harder still when the lower nature is reinforced by the civilization around us. Materialism is one of its sharpest expressions today: a culture organized around getting, consuming, and being seen, with the institutions and incentives to match. Shoghi Effendi names this pattern as a civilizational condition, not just a personal one:

> The materialistic civilization of our age has so much absorbed the energy and interest of mankind, that people in general no longer feel the necessity of raising themselves above the forces and conditions of their daily material existence... The universal crisis affecting mankind is, therefore, essentially spiritual in its causes. [1]

Elsewhere, Shoghi Effendi names the personal stakes of this condition directly:

> How to attain spirituality is, indeed, a question to which every young man and woman must sooner or later try to find a satisfactory answer. It is precisely because no such satisfactory reply has been given or found, that modern youth finds itself bewildered, and is being consequently carried away by the materialistic forces that are so powerfully undermining the foundation of man's moral and spiritual life. [2]

The lack of spirituality is not just a feature of civilizations. It is felt in our own lives. We can know intellectually that God created us out of love, that traditions across history insist God is near, and still feel an inexplicable distance. So where is God in the day-to-day? And what is the work that closes the gap?

Abdu'l-Baha offers a powerful image for these questions. Imagine the human heart as a mirror, and God as the sun. The sun shines on everything around it, but a mirror reflects the light only when it is both polished and turned toward the sun. A dusty mirror reflects very little, even when the sun is unchanged. A mirror facing the wrong direction reflects nothing at all. The light is not the variable here. The condition and orientation of the mirror determine whether the light reflects at all.

> "The most important thing is to polish the mirrors of hearts in order that they may become illumined and receptive of the divine light. One heart may possess the capacity of the polished mirror; another, be covered and obscured by the dust and dross of this world." [3]

The light is constant; we were made to reflect it. The work of a spiritual life is not to manufacture light we do not have. It is to reflect the light already provided to the world. As Baha'u'llah says, *"Thou art My lamp and My light is in thee. Get thou from it thy radiance."* [4] The capacity is already in us.

But the image is also a reminder. It is natural for dust to accumulate on anything. Removing dust from objects is a deliberate action; it is the development of spirituality as a driving force in our lives.

Two kinds of work emerge from this image. The first is orientation: turning the mirror toward the sun, so we can reflect the light of God in the world. The second is polishing: the development of our souls to reflect the light more clearly.

Neither happens automatically. Both are best developed through positive habits.

## Section 2: A Recipe, Not a Rulebook

### Questions for Reflection

*(to be drafted after both reading passages are approved)*

### Reading

So what does the development of spirituality look like day to day? Largely, the habits can be grouped into those focused on orienting the mirror toward the sun, and those focused on polishing away the dust from our hearts. The dust gathers daily, the orientation can drift daily, so the practice must be daily too. Spiritual development is not a single decision or a one-time conversion. It is a rhythm, a returning, again and again, to the practices that keep our hearts oriented toward the light.

The good news is that we have not been left to figure this out alone. Baha'u'llah revealed a body of teachings designed precisely for this work. And He said something striking about how to read them. Within the very book of His laws, the Kitab-i-Aqdas, He writes:

> Think not that We have revealed unto you a mere code of laws. Nay, rather, We have unsealed the choice Wine with the fingers of might and power. To this beareth witness that which the Pen of Revelation hath revealed. Meditate upon this, O men of insight! [5]

This is a deliberate reframing. Baha'u'llah is not handing us a list of rules to obey. He is saying that what He has revealed is something more like an unsealed treasure: a recipe for spiritual development so we do not need to discover it entirely through trial and error. The laws are not separate from God's love; the laws provided are its expression. Abdu'l-Baha makes the same point in different language: *"His laws all grant liberation rather than restriction; they confer freedom rather than limitation; they impart joy and radiance rather than constraint."* [6]

The teachings name several daily and yearly practices that do two kinds of work together: they orient the mirror toward the sun, and they remove the dust that has gathered. Each one matters; the combination is what the Faith offers.

At the heart of the recipe is prayer. The Faith treats prayer as the practice that nourishes the soul the way food nourishes the body, and the act in which we most directly meet God. Shoghi Effendi puts it plainly:

> "It is the soul of man that has first to be fed. And this spiritual nourishment prayer can best provide... the core of religious faith is that mystic feeling that unites man with God." [1]

He returns to this point with even greater urgency:

> The believers, particularly the young ones, should therefore realize fully the necessity of praying. For prayer is absolutely indispensable to their inner spiritual development, and this, as already stated, is the very foundation and purpose of the religion of God. [1]

Prayer is the closest practice we have to that meeting. It is how we orient the mirror toward the sun, day after day.

Around this anchor, Baha'u'llah revealed other practices to develop spiritually. He calls us to immerse ourselves in the sacred writings, to feed the soul on God's words. He gives us daily prayer - many revealed prayers to use to communicate with God and also an obligation to say one of three prayers each day. He gives us a yearly fast of nineteen days, abstaining from food and drink between dawn and sunset, as a reminder of our spiritual nature. He calls us to bring ourselves to account each day, to honestly review our actions. He elevates daily work itself to the level of worship when it is performed with care and a desire to serve. And He calls us to direct service of others, to people in our communities, to those in need, to the world. 

All this work is gradual. Daily effort, oriented in the right direction, compounds. Baha'u'llah calls us to *"Let each morn be better than its eve and each morrow richer than its yesterday."* [8]

We can put this practice into action through the instruction of Abdu'l-Baha, and it can begin tomorrow morning:

> Every day, in the morning when arising one should compare today with yesterday and see in what condition you are. If you see your belief is stronger and your heart more occupied with God and your love increased and your freedom from the world greater then thank God and ask for the increase of these qualities. [9]

This is the shape of a spiritual life, day by day. None of it is sustained by us alone. Baha'u'llah's teachings hold that even our turning toward the light draws something forward. When we orient ourselves, even imperfectly, divine assistance is already there to meet us. The light is constant. What changes, day by day, is us.
