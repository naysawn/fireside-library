# Spiritual Development

## Section 1: Our Twofold Nature

### Questions for Reflection

*(to be drafted after both reading passages are approved)*

### Reading

The Baha'i teachings describe human beings as having a twofold nature. We have a higher, spiritual nature, capable of reflecting God's attributes such as kindness, generosity, justice, and love. And we have an egotistical nature, pulled toward self-interest and the satisfaction of immediate desires.

The lower nature goes by many names. Baha'i writings call it the insistent self, the ego, even the whisperings of Satan. Left unchecked, Shoghi Effendi tells us, it can become *"a monster of selfishness, brutality, lust and so on."*

The ego is the animal self. It is the part of us full of selfish desires. Baha'i teachings treat it as part of our inherited nature, not something to be cast out. Subduing it requires sustained effort through prayer, obedience to God's laws, and honest self-examination. The work is never finished.

Spiritual development is the ongoing work of subordinating the lower nature to the higher. In the Baha'i view, this is the central work of human life.

> The ego is the animal in us, the heritage of the flesh which is full of selfish desires. By obeying the laws of God, seeking to live the life laid down in our teachings, and prayer and struggle, we can subdue our egos. We call people 'saints' who have achieved the highest degree of mastery over their ego. [1]

The ego and the love of worldly things are part of the dust that obscures us from reflecting the light of God. Abdu'l-Baha offers an analogy for this work. He writes:

> The light of the sun becomes apparent in each object according to the capacity of that object. The difference is simply one of degree and receptivity. The stone would be a recipient only to a limited extent; another created thing might be as a mirror wherein the sun is fully reflected; but the same light shines upon both.
>
> The most important thing is to polish the mirrors of hearts in order that they may become illumined and receptive of the divine light. One heart may possess the capacity of the polished mirror; another, be covered and obscured by the dust and dross of this world. Although the same Sun is shining upon both, in the mirror which is polished, pure and sanctified you may behold the Sun in all its fullness, glory and power, revealing its majesty and effulgence; but in the mirror which is rusted and obscured there is no capacity for reflection, although so far as the Sun itself is concerned it is shining thereon and is neither lessened nor deprived. Therefore, our duty lies in seeking to polish the mirrors of our hearts in order that we shall become reflectors of that light and recipients of the divine bounties which may be fully revealed through them. [2]

The light and love of God is constant. We were made to reflect it. But we cannot reflect it unless we are oriented to the light and able to reflect it in the world. 

Baha'u'llah says:

> Thou art My lamp and My light is in thee. Get thou from it thy radiance and seek none other than Me. For I have created thee rich and have bountifully shed My favor upon thee. [3]

Baha'u'llah revealed a body of teachings precisely for this work: the polishing and the turning. He warned against reading them as a list of rules:

> Think not that We have revealed unto you a mere code of laws. Nay, rather, We have unsealed the choice Wine with the fingers of might and power. To this beareth witness that which the Pen of Revelation hath revealed. Meditate upon this, O men of insight! [4]

The daily shape of polishing a mirror and turning it toward the sun is the work of the next section.

## Section 2: Developing Spirituality

### Questions for Reflection

*(to be drafted after both reading passages are approved)*

### Reading

So what does the development of spirituality look like day to day? Baha'u'llah's guidance is a recipe, not a list of rules. As Abdu'l-Baha puts it, *"His laws all grant liberation rather than restriction; they confer freedom rather than limitation; they impart joy and radiance rather than constraint."* [5] The recipe has two kinds of work, the same two the mirror image asked of us. The first is orientation: turning the mirror toward the light. The second is the refinement of our character: polishing the mirror clear so it can reflect.

#### Orientation

Orientation is the daily work of remembering: we are spiritual beings, dependent on God, made to reflect what shines on us. Baha'u'llah names our condition with two analogies. He compares us to leaves on a tree, which *"depend upon it for their existence, yet remain oblivious of their root and origin,"* and to fish in the deep, whose *"life dependeth upon the water, and yet they remain unaware of that which… sustaineth their very existence."* These analogies, He says, are given *"that perchance the people may turn unto Him."* [6] Three practices anchor this turning: recognition, prayer, and immersion in the sacred writings.

1. Prayer is at the center. Shoghi Effendi puts it plainly: *"It is the soul of man that has first to be fed. And this spiritual nourishment prayer can best provide… the core of religious faith is that mystic feeling that unites man with God."* [7] Baha'u'llah revealed many prayers for daily life and the obligation to recite one of three each day.

completes the turning: each morning and evening, we are called to feed the soul on the words Baha'u'llah revealed, keeping the orientation we depend on alive.

#### Refinement of Character

Refinement is the slower work of building the character that can reflect what shines on us. Baha'u'llah names this as the very object of His revelation:

> The object of every Revelation is to effect a transformation in the whole character of mankind, a transformation that shall manifest itself, both outwardly and inwardly. [8]

When the lower nature is in the lead, divine qualities like patience, generosity, justice and honesty get obscured. Refinement is the daily work of bringing them forward. Four practices serve it.

1. **The fast.** Each year, for nineteen days, we abstain from food and drink between dawn and sunset. The practice symbolizes detachment from the things of this world and reminds us of our spiritual nature and our dependence on God.

2. **Daily self-accounting.** Baha'u'llah counsels us: *"Bring thyself to account each day ere thou art summoned to a reckoning; for death, unheralded, shall come upon thee and thou shalt be called to give account for thy deeds."* [9] Regular self-examination keeps us honest about our spiritual progress and our finite time on this earth.

3. **Daily work in the service of others.** The Baha'i teachings elevate daily work to a sacred dimension. As Abdu'l-Baha explains, *"all effort and exertion put forth by man from the fullness of his heart is worship, if it is prompted by the highest motives and the will to do service to humanity."* [10] Whether we are teachers, farmers, artists, or healthcare workers, our work becomes worship when performed with excellence and in the spirit of service. Through it, we develop virtues like diligence, integrity, and care for those we serve.

4. **Sharing the Faith with others.** When something nourishes our own souls, we offer it to others. Baha'u'llah exhorts us to become *"a source of all goodness unto men, and an example of uprightness to mankind."* [11]

This work is gradual. Baha'u'llah calls us to *"let each morn be better than its eve and each morrow richer than its yesterday."* [12] And Abdu'l-Baha gives us a practice that can begin tomorrow:

> Every day, in the morning when arising one should compare today with yesterday and see in what condition you are. If you see your belief is stronger and your heart more occupied with God and your love increased and your freedom from the world greater then thank God and ask for the increase of these qualities. [13]

This is the shape of a spiritual life, day by day. The work is ours, but never ours alone. When we turn, even imperfectly, grace is already there to meet us. The light has not moved. We have.
