Bahá'u'lláh frames His laws not as a mere regulatory code but as the unsealed "choice Wine" of divine revelation — a living draught that discloses hidden spiritual truths, transforms human character, and constitutes the very "secrets of existence." The imagery drawn from the Kitáb-i-Aqdas is dense and multisensory: wine, fragrance, ocean, lamps, keys, and strongholds all serve to convey that these ordinances are ontological realities about human nature, not arbitrary commands.

EVIDENCE
The Core Declaration: "Not a mere code of laws"
The pivotal passage appears in the Kitáb-i-Aqdas and is quoted across nearly every major commentary in these sources: "Think not that We have revealed unto you a mere code of laws. Nay, rather, We have unsealed the choice Wine with the fingers of might and power. To this beareth witness that which the Pen of Revelation hath revealed. Meditate upon this, O men of insight!"
1
KAQ This statement is deliberately paradoxical — Bahá'u'lláh issues it within the very book of laws, immediately reframing what those laws fundamentally are. The Universal House of Justice explicitly draws out this context, observing that through His revelation "the principles required for the collective coming of age of the human race have been invested with the one power capable of penetrating to the roots of human motivation and of altering behaviour." 
2
OC

What the Wine Actually Is: Unsealing Hidden Truths
The notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas clarify the full metaphysical weight of the wine image. The Qur'án had promised the righteous "the choice sealed wine"; Bahá'u'lláh declares He has now unsealed it. In His Tablets, He "identifies the 'choice Wine' with His Revelation whose 'musk-laden fragrance' has been wafted 'upon all created things'" and states that those who quaff it can "discern the splendors of the light of divine unity" and "grasp the essential purpose underlying the Scriptures of God." 
3
KA This is the "secrets of existence" register you are asking about — the wine is not pleasant feeling but illuminated comprehension of reality itself.

Bahá'u'lláh amplifies this in a Tablet collected in Gleanings: "I have, moreover, with the hand of divine power, unsealed the choice wine of My Revelation, and have wafted its holy, its hidden, and musk-laden fragrance upon all created things. Who else but yourselves is to be blamed if ye choose to remain unendowed with so great an outpouring of God's transcendent and all-encompassing grace?" 
4
BW The emphasis on hiddenness — holy, hidden, musk-laden — anchors the laws as previously concealed truths now disclosed for the first time.

The Cluster of Sensory Metaphors Surrounding the Laws
The choice-wine image does not stand alone; it sits within a remarkable cluster of metaphors that together frame the laws as living, fragrant, intoxicating reality. Immediately preceding the "choice Wine" declaration, Bahá'u'lláh states: "From My laws the sweet-smelling savour of My garment can be smelled" 
5
PR — an allusion to the Joseph story, where Jacob recognizes his lost son by the fragrance of his garment. The notes explain this metaphor "is frequently used in the Bahá'í Writings to refer to the recognition of the Manifestation of God and His Revelation." 
6
KA Recognition of the laws is thus recognition of the Revealer Himself.

The preceding lines intensify the intoxication image: "By My life! He who hath drunk the choice wine of fairness from the hands of My bountiful favour, will circle around My commandments that shine above the Dayspring of My creation." 
7
BW And before that, the commandments are called "the lamps of My loving providence among My servants, and the keys of My mercy for My creatures," with the promise that "were any man to taste the sweetness of the words which the lips of the All-Merciful have willed to utter, he would, though the treasures of the earth be in his possession, renounce them one and all, that he might vindicate the truth of even one of His commandments."
8
PR The Ruhi curriculum adds two further images from Bahá'u'lláh: "the laws of God are like unto the ocean and the children of men as fish" 
9
RB and "each one of the ordinances We have revealed is a mighty stronghold for the preservation of the world of being." 
10
RB

Laws as Statements About the Reality of Human Nature
This imagery carries a specific philosophical argument: the laws are not impositions from outside human nature but revelations of what human nature actually is. The House of Justice articulates this directly: "In recognizing the Manifestation of God for today, a believer also acknowledges that His laws and exhortations express truths about the nature of the human being and the purpose of existence; they raise human consciousness, increase understanding, lift the standard of personal conduct, and provide the means for society to progress." 
11
FA The One Common Faith essay makes the same point more concretely: "For those who have recognized Him, equality of men and women is not a sociological postulate, but revealed truth about human nature." 
12
OC The laws are thus epistemological — they disclose what is real — not merely ethical.

Liberty and Transformation as the Fruit
The wine metaphor culminates in a counterintuitive claim about freedom: "True liberty consisteth in man's submission unto My commandments, little as ye know it. Were men to observe that which We have sent down unto them from the Heaven of Revelation, they would, of a certainty, attain unto perfect liberty." 
13
RB The House of Justice frames this as the distinction between obedience to an "arbitrary and dictatorial regimen" versus a call that "leads to true freedom." 
14
FA 'Abdu'l-Bahá reinforces this: "His laws all grant liberation rather than restriction; they confer freedom rather than limitation; they impart joy and radiance rather than constraint." 
15
RB

Bahá'u'lláh's declared object is total transformation: "The object of every Revelation is to effect a transformation in the whole character of mankind, a transformation that shall manifest itself, both outwardly and inwardly." 
16
FA The wine metaphor serves this transformative claim — wine changes the one who drinks it; the laws, properly received, remake the person and society together.

The Sealed Wine in Communal Experience
The wine image extends beyond the laws to the entire Revelation received by the community. In one prayer, Bahá'u'lláh gives thanks that His loved ones "have drunk the choice wine of revelation and inspiration from the hand of their Lord, the Compassionate, the Merciful." 
17
TA In a festival tablet, He declares: "Thus hath the Tongue of the Ancient of Days spoken on this Day wherein the choice wine hath been unsealed." 
18
DR The unsealing is thus an event in sacred history — a moment of cosmic disclosure — not only a metaphor for personal piety.
