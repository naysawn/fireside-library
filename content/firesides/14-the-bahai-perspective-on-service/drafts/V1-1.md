# The Baha'i Perspective on Service

## Section 1: Created to Manifest God

### Opening Questions

1. Think of someone you've known whom you admire for how much they give to others. Let's honor them: tell us about them, and what it is you admire.

2. When you think of a person's life that has really mattered, whose life comes to mind? Why does it stand out to you?

### Reading

Throughout history, humanity has pondered the fundamental questions of existence: Who are we? Why are we here? 

<!-- BEAT 1 — Made in the image of God: built to reflect His qualities -->

Baha'u'llah teaches that we are spiritual beings, created out of divine love and in the image and likeness of God. 

> Veiled in My immemorial being and in the ancient eternity of My essence, I knew My love for thee; therefore I created thee, have engraved on thee Mine image and revealed to thee My beauty. (Baha'u'lláh, The Hidden Words)

This does not mean God has a body; Baha'u'llah teaches that God is beyond anything we can picture or conceive. It means that each of us was given the capacity to reflect God's own qualities: love, honesty, courage, compassion, generosity. We are like mirrors, made to catch a light that is not our own and pass it on.

<!-- BEAT 2 — Two natures: higher/divine vs lower/ego -->
But there are two sides to us. Alongside that higher, God-reflecting nature runs a lower one: the ego, turned in on its own wants. Much of a human life is the quiet contest between the two, between the self that gives and the self that grasps.

<!-- BEAT 3 — Acting virtuously manifests the divine nature into the world -->
Every time the higher nature wins out, every time we actually act with love or patience or honesty, something of God becomes visible in the world. The virtue is not merely ours; it is the divine nature within us, showing through.

<!-- BEAT 4 — So the good is God's -->
Which is why Baha'u'llah can say, *"Every good thing is of God, and every evil thing is from yourselves."* The good we manage is never finally our own invention. It is God's, reflected through us; only the failures are ours alone.

<!-- BEAT 5 — Created to know and worship God -->
If that is so, the most important thing a person can do is to become someone through whom that goodness freely flows. The writings name this as the very reason we were made. A short prayer many Baha'is say each day puts it in a single line: *"Thou hast created me to know Thee and to worship Thee."*

<!-- BEAT 6 — Worship IS service: our acts become prayers -->
And worship, in this Faith, is not mainly something done in a quiet room with the eyes closed. To worship God is to let His qualities live in what we do. *"Service is prayer,"* Abdu'l-Baha taught: an ordinary act of kindness, offered with love, is itself a form of worship.

<!-- BEAT 7 — SYNTHESIS: our whole purpose is acts of service that channel God's love to the world; love gives life -->
Put the pieces together and one picture emerges. We were made in God's image to reflect His qualities, and the highest use of that gift is to serve, to become a clear channel through which God's love reaches the world. So you might say the whole purpose of a human life is just this: acts of service that carry God's love out to others. And that love is no mere sentiment; it is the most creative force there is. As Abdu'l-Baha said,

> Love gives life to the lifeless. Love lights a flame in the heart that is cold. Love brings hope to the hopeless and gladdens the hearts of the sorrowful. In the world of existence there is indeed no greater power than the power of love. (Abdu'l-Bahá, Paris Talks)

Which raises the question Section 2 takes up: what does carrying that love actually look like, day to day, with real people?

### Questions for Discussion

1. The reading says the good we do isn't finally ours, it's God's, reflected through us. Does that lift a burden off you, or take something away? How does it actually sit?

2. The reading claims that to serve others is itself a form of worship, that *"Service is prayer."* How does that concept sit with you?
