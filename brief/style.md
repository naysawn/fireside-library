# Writing Style Guide

## Voice and Readability

- Write at a 10th-grade reading level. A high school student should be able to follow every sentence without re-reading.
- Use analogies drawn from nature and the physical world to explain spiritual or abstract concepts. Example: a child develops in the womb the qualities needed for this life, just as we develop spiritual qualities in this world that are needed for the next.
- The tone is warm and conversational, like a thoughtful friend explaining something, not a lecture.
- Do not use contractions in prose (write "do not" instead of "don't," "it is" instead of "it's").

## Avoiding AI Tells

The finished text should never read as though AI wrote it. Specific patterns to avoid:

- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or semicolons instead. Em dashes are a telltale sign of AI-generated text.
- **No "not X, not Y, but Z" constructions.** Rephrase to state the point directly.
- **Avoid overused AI words and phrases:** "delve," "tapestry," "multifaceted," "it's worth noting," "let's unpack," "Indeed," "In essence," "Moreover," "Furthermore."
- **Vary sentence structure.** Avoid predictable rhythms and overly parallel constructions. Read passages aloud to check for a natural cadence.
- This list will be updated as new patterns are identified.

## Quotations

### Inline quotes
- Short quotations (generally one sentence or a phrase) are woven into the prose and italicized.
- Example: Abdu'l-Baha describes prayer as *"the essential spiritual conversation of the soul with its Maker."* [1]

### Block quotes
- Longer quotations (two or more sentences) are formatted as block quotes.
- When drawing multiple phrases from the same source in a single paragraph, combine them into one block quote instead. Use an ellipsis (`…`) to connect the relevant passages together.
- Each module should contain at least 3-4 block quotes.
- Block quotes may stand alone or be introduced by surrounding prose. Both are acceptable.
- Example:

> "Know thou, verily, it is becoming in a weak one to supplicate to the Strong One, and it behooveth a seeker of bounty to beseech the Glorious Bountiful One. When one supplicates to his Lord, turns to Him and seeks bounty from His Ocean, this supplication brings light to his heart, illumination to his sight, life to his soul and exaltation to his being." [2]

### Citations
- All quotations, whether inline or block, are cited with a numbered reference in the text (e.g., [1]).
- A citations section at the end of each module lists the full source for each reference.
- Example:

**Citations**
1. Universal House of Justice, message to the Baha'is in Iran, 18 December 2014.
2. Abdu'l-Baha, cited in J. E. Esslemont, *Baha'u'llah and the New Era*, 5th rev. ed. (Wilmette: Baha'i Publishing Trust, 1987), p. 93.

## Spelling

- Write **Baha'i**, **Baha'u'llah**, **Abdu'l-Baha**, **Shoghi Effendi** with standard apostrophes, without diacritical marks (no dots, macrons, or special characters).
- This applies throughout all text: readings, questions, headers, and citations.

## Formatting

- Italics are acceptable for emphasis and for inline quotations.
- Avoid bold in reading passages.
- Use smart/curly quotes (" ") rather than straight quotes (" ").

## Acceptance Criteria

Before a module is considered ready for review, check against this list:

- [ ] Could a 10th-grade student read and understand every sentence without difficulty?
- [ ] Are complex or abstract ideas explained with at least one analogy drawn from nature or everyday experience?
- [ ] Does the text avoid em dashes, "not X but Y" constructions, and AI-indicator words?
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like a human wrote it, or does it have the flat rhythm of AI output?
- [ ] Are there at least 3-4 block quotes from the source material?
- [ ] Are all quotations cited with numbered references, and is a citations section at the end?
- [ ] Are Baha'i names and terms spelled without diacritical marks?
- [ ] Is the tone warm and conversational, not academic or preachy?
- [ ] Do opening questions draw on universal experience without assuming Baha'i knowledge?
- [ ] Do discussion questions open genuine exploration rather than leading to predetermined answers?
